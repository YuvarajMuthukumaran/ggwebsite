Comprehensive Mental Health Services

Areas of care, matched to severity and delivered by a team rather than one clinician, with treatment adjusted as things change.

Areas of care

De-addiction

Treatment for alcohol and drug dependence, from medically supervised withdrawal through to relapse prevention.

The emphasis is less on detoxification itself than on what holds afterwards. Alcohol , Drugs , Withdrawal management , Smoking cessation→

Dual diagnosis

A substance use disorder alongside a second psychiatric condition.

Treating either one in isolation tends to fail, which is why these cases are managed together and over a longer horizon. Comorbidity , Integrated care , Long-term review→

Schizophrenia

A chronic and severe illness that asks for continuity more than intensity.

Acute episodes are stabilised; the longer work is rehabilitation and a return to ordinary function. Acute care , Long-term rehabilitation , Family support→

Bipolar disorder

Formerly called manic-depressive illness.

Care is built around recognising the shape of a particular person’s cycle, and around staying well between episodes. Mood stabilisation , Relapse prevention , Psychoeducation→

Depression

A mood disorder, not a mood.

Assessment distinguishes depressive illness from the ordinary low periods it is often mistaken for, and treatment is matched to severity. Medication , CBT , Deep TMS→

Anxiety & OCD

Anxiety disorders, obsessive-compulsive disorder and the stress states that sit beneath them.

Treated with a combination of medication and structured behavioural work.CBT , Deep TMS , Biofeedback , Stress management→

Personality disorders

Borderline, narcissistic and schizotypal presentations, where the difficulty is enduring rather than episodic.

The work is slower, more relational, and measured over months. Borderline , Narcissistic , Schizotypal , DBT→

Eating disorders

A group of disorders in which a person is preoccupied by food and body image and cannot concentrate on anything else.

They are serious, challenging to treat and can be life-threatening if left unattended. The main types are anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa and binge-eating disorder. Psychotherapy , Medication , Family counselling , Nutritionist→

How treatment works

  1. 01

    A plan matched to severity

    Medication, CBT or DBT, Deep TMS and biofeedback, delivered by a team rather than one clinician and adjusted as things change.

  2. 02

    After the first phase

    For chronic illness and addiction, the months after the acute phase matter most. Aftercare, review and rehabilitation continue well past the point where the crisis has passed.

  3. 03

    Family involvement

    For long-term conditions such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and addiction, family support is part of care, and the team works with families on psychoeducation and aftercare.
