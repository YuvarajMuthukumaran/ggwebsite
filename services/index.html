OPD or admission

When is admission needed?

Most people are treated as outpatients and never need a bed. Admission is for when someone cannot be kept safe or cannot be treated at home, and it is always a conversation with the family first.

Most of the time

Outpatient (OPD)

You come for a consultation, leave with a plan, and come back for review. Medication, therapy and follow-up all happen while you carry on with work, study and family.

  • A first assessment and diagnosis
  • Medication started and adjusted
  • Therapy, and family sessions where they help
  • Review appointments, in person or online

Where: Sarvapriya Vihar, New Delhi, Sector 64 Gurugram, or online.

When OPD is not enough

Admission

A bed is considered when the risk is too high to manage at home, when withdrawal needs medical supervision, or when someone has stopped eating, sleeping or taking treatment.

  • Risk of harm to themselves or someone else
  • Detox that needs monitoring
  • An episode that has not settled on outpatient treatment
  • No one at home able to supervise care safely

Where: Tulasi Psychiatric and Rehabilitation Centre, Sector 64 (Acute care) and Tulasi Home, Mehrauli (Long-term rehabilitation).

For families

What families should expect

For addiction and serious mental illness, the family is usually the one who calls. You can book the consultation, come to it, and be part of the plan.

  • You can ask for the consultation on someone else’s behalf
  • The team explains the diagnosis and the plan to you, with consent
  • Visiting and involvement continue through an admission
  • Aftercare and relapse prevention are planned with you

Talk it through with the team

This page describes how care is organised. Whether admission is needed is a clinical decision, made with you after an assessment.

How treatment works

A plan matched to severity

Medication, CBT or DBT, Deep TMS and biofeedback, delivered by a team rather than one clinician and adjusted as things change.

After the first phase

For chronic illness and addiction, the months after the acute phase matter most. Aftercare, review and rehabilitation continue well past the point where the crisis has passed.

Family involvement

For long-term conditions such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and addiction, family support is part of care, and the team works with families on psychoeducation and aftercare.
