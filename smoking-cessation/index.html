Patient-Centered Care: dTMS

Deep Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (dTMS) is a neurostimulation technique that uses electromagnetic coils to target specific areas of the brain.

In traditional TMS, a coil is placed on the patient’s scalp to stimulate the brain’s outer regions. dTMS, as the name suggests, can penetrate deeper into the brain, reaching subcortical regions.

Smoking addiction involves complex neural pathways and reward systems in the brain.

dTMS may influence these pathways, particularly those related to cravings, withdrawal symptoms, and reward responses.

By modulating specific brain regions, dTMS could help individuals reduce their cravings for nicotine and manage the symptoms associated with nicotine withdrawal.

Frequently asked questions

Smoking cessation by Dr. Gorav Gupta

Can I quit smoking without professional help?

While some individuals successfully quit smoking on their own, many find it challenging due to nicotine addiction and the psychological aspects of smoking. Professional help can significantly increase your chances of success. We offer evidence-based treatments and support tailored to your specific needs.

What are the most effective methods for quitting smoking?

The most effective methods often combine behavioral counseling, medication, and support. Behavioral therapies, like cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), can help you identify and change smoking triggers and behaviors. Medications, such as nicotine replacement therapy or prescription medications, can reduce cravings and withdrawal symptoms.

How long does it take to overcome withdrawal symptoms?

Withdrawal symptoms can vary from person to person. They are typically most intense in the first few days after quitting and gradually subside. Most withdrawal symptoms significantly improve within a few weeks to a few months. However, it's essential to have a support system in place to manage these symptoms effectively.

What role does mental health play in smoking cessation?

Mental health is closely linked to smoking cessation. Individuals often use smoking as a coping mechanism for stress, anxiety, or depression. It's essential to address these underlying mental health issues during the quitting process. Dual diagnosis treatment can help individuals manage their mental health challenges while working toward smoking cessation.
