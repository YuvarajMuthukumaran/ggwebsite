What are Obsessions?

  • Obsessions can be defined as unwelcome, obtrusive thoughts, pictures, ideas, or desires that cause serious distress.
  • They are main defining characteristic of OCD.
  • These include unwelcome fixations on bodily sensations, intrusive ideas of hurting others, uncertainty about one’s own identity, and a myriad of other issues.

What are compulsions?

  • Compulsions can be defined as actions or thoughts performed in an effort to lessen the anguish brought on by the obsessions.
  • Compulsions are frequently performed in an effort to identify and eliminate a perceived threat in order to stop a feared outcome from happening.
  • Compulsions may temporarily alleviate the suffering brought on by obsessions, but they also promote the idea that they effectively countered a real threat or danger, which makes obsessions more likely to recur.
  • These may include counting things, washing hands frequently, cleaning excessively or checking if doors and windows are closed, etc.

TREATMENT FOR OCD BY Dr. GORAV GUPTA

OCD is not a mental or physical health condition. OCD is a perceptual problem associated with problems with cognitive function and cognition. Therefore, it’s crucial to set priorities and take your OCD patient to an OCD psychiatrist or therapist who can assist with cognitive restructuring the patient’s mind. The first line of defence for treatment is psychological intervention as opposed to seeing a psychiatrist or physician. OCD is not entirely cured by medicine. Although, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved many antidepressants for the treatment of OCD.

  • Anafranil (clomipramine) for both adults and kids 10 years or more
  • Fluoxetine (Prozac) for both children and adults 7 years or more
  • Fluvoxamine for both children and adults 8 years old and up
  • Paxil, Pexeva, and paroxetine are solely for adults.
  • Sertraline (Zoloft) is recommended for adults and kids ages 6 and older.

Psychotherapy and drugs may not always be sufficient to control OCD symptoms. Other methods could be provided in cases that are resistant to treatment programmes for intensive outpatient and inpatient therapy.

ERP or exposure and response prevention technique:

For best OCD treatment in Delhi patients should involve Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP) and/or medication. The majority of people with OCD (about 7 out of 10) will benefit from either medication or ERP. People with OCD who struggle to function due to the severity of their symptoms may benefit from comprehensive treatment programmes that emphasise ERP therapy concepts. These courses usually run for several weeks. In ERP, a person who experiences intrusive thoughts is deliberately exposed to circumstances that trigger those ideas; but, instead of acting on their compulsions, they are required to sit with the thoughts. The purpose of ERP is to convince the user that their fears about what might occur when they have an intrusive thought are unlikely to occur. For instance, someone who fears stabbing someone could avoid or store blades, or engage in compulsive behaviour when around them.

TMS or transcranial magnetic stimulation:

When conventional therapy methods have failed to control OCD in adults aged 22 to 68, the FDA approved a specialised device (Deep Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation). dTMS is a non-invasive treatment for OCD symptoms that stimulates brain nerve cells with magnetic fields. An electromagnetic coil is applied to your scalp near your forehead during a dTMS session. Your brain’s nerve cells are stimulated by a magnetic pulse delivered by the electromagnet.

Frequently asked questions

OCD Psychiatrists

Is OCD a psychiatric condition?

OCD is not a mental or physical health condition. OCD is a “perceptual problem” which deals with perceptions of the patient and is usually associated with problems with cognitive function and cognition.

How can one overcome the intrusive thoughts or obsessions?

To overcome intrusive thoughts or obsessions, we must confront all of our obsessional ideas with reasoning and a framework of logical patterns.

Can a psychiatrist diagnose OCD?

Yes, a psychiatrist can diagnose your OCD condition based on your past history, your behaviour and cognition.

Has anyone ever been fully cured of OCD?

There is currently no known medical treatment that will permanently end OCD. However, there is a particularly effective form of cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) called exposure and response (ERP) therapy for OCD and another cutting edge technology called deep(TMS) which may help you a long way in eradicating OCD symptoms.
