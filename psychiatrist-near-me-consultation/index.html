How do I know if I need to see a psychiatrist?

A psychiatrist may be able to help you if you are dealing with severe or persistent emotional problems, behavioural issues, or signs of mental disorders including stress, anxiety, depression, mood swings, or hallucinations and delusions. They are able to evaluate your health, make a diagnosis, and suggest the best course of action for normalising your mental health.

Remember, it is not a sign of weakness but a show of both strength and humility if you are willing to reach out for help. Continuing negative behaviours or thought patterns can be detrimental and have long-lasting consequences. Early intervention and a willingness to get a mental health professional’s guidance is the best thing you can do for yourself and your loved ones.

What are the common causes of mental illness?
  • Genetics (hereditary)
  • Environmental stressors
  • Chemical imbalances inside the brain
  • Untreated brain injury, brain tumour or encephalitis
  • Alcohol and/or drug abuse
  • Brain damage in the prenatal or fetal stage, or during birth
  • Postpartum depression
  • Poor nutrition
  • Exposure to toxins

Who is a psychiatrist, and what do they do?

A psychiatrist is a doctor with expertise in mental health. They use a combination of medical, psychological and social therapies to diagnose, treat and manage mental illness and to look after the emotional well-being of their patients. In contrast to other mental health professionals, a psychiatrist is the only professional who may prescribe medicines, along with psychotherapies and other procedures. Other mental health professionals can only help you with psychotherapy and talk therapy.

How would a psychiatrist help?

A psychiatrist is a fully qualified and trained medical doctor who deals with the evaluation, diagnosis, prevention and treatment of different mental, behavioural and emotional disorders.

What treatment options do psychiatrists offer?

Pharmacotherapy (medication management), psychotherapy (talk therapy), cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) and other empirically supported therapies are just a few of the treatment choices psychiatrists can offer. They might also advocate support networks, coping mechanisms and lifestyle adjustments. In combination, they may also advise treatments such as Deep Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation therapy, bio-feedback therapy and Virtual Reality therapy.

Do I need counselling or therapy?

If you are struggling with emotional issues, stress, or mental health concerns such as anxiety or depression, counselling with a mental health therapist or psychologist could help. Therapy provides a safe space to explore feelings, develop coping strategies and gain insight into your challenges. If you are unsure, speaking with a mental health professional can help determine whether therapy is right for you.

What is the best treatment for OCD?

The best treatment for OCD typically involves a combination of cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), specifically Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP), and medication. Consulting a psychiatrist and a clinical psychologist first is the recommended course of action. Dr. Gorav Gupta treats OCD at our consulting rooms and online. Read more on OCD treatment.

Yes. Through a rehabilitation plan, a psychiatrist can help you manage and treat alcoholism.
