Popular questions

Do I need a psychiatrist?

Remember, it is not a sign of weakness but a show of both strength and humility if you are willing to reach out for help. Continuing negative behaviours or thought patterns can be detrimental and have long-lasting consequences. Early intervention and a willingness to get a mental health professional’s guidance is the best thing you can do for yourself and your loved ones.

What is the difference between a psychiatrist and a psychologist?

Psychiatrists and psychologists are both mental health professionals, but they differ in their approach to treating mental health problems.

What treatment options do psychiatrists offer?

Pharmacotherapy (medication management), psychotherapy (talk therapy), cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) and other empirically supported therapies are just a few of the treatment choices psychiatrists can offer. They might also advocate support networks, coping mechanisms and lifestyle adjustments. In combination, they may also advise treatments such as Deep Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation therapy, bio-feedback therapy and Virtual Reality therapy.

More in “Treatment and therapy” →

Can a psychiatrist help with alcoholism?

Yes. Through a rehabilitation plan, a psychiatrist can help you manage and treat alcoholism. With the help of a counselling psychologist, mental health therapist, clinical psychologist and behavioural therapist, they work on a long-term, personalised plan for a condition like alcoholism.
