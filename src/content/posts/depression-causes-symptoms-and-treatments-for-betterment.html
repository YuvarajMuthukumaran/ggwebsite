Exploring the causes- Why does depression occur?

Depression is quite a common condition nowadays, but its onset in a person can be traced back to a variety of factors.
Some of us may be triggered by lifestyle issues, some may hold a genetic capacity for it and some may simply acquire it as a result of another condition. Let’s discuss how they trigger our depression.

  • Brain chemistry- The body is regulated by a variety of neurotransmitters. When these neurotransmitters experience an imbalance, usually serotonin and dopamine, it triggers the neural pathways which result in triggering depression.
  • Genetic makeup- The risk of developing depression is thrice as likely for a person who has a first degree relative (immediate family/sibling) who suffers from it.
  • Traumatic life events- A person who has gone through a personal loss, physical/mental trauma or survived a difficult or stressful situation is more prone to succumb to depression.
  • Medical complications- pregnancy, diabetes, chronic pain or PTSD can cause a person to slip into depression or raise chances for its development. Specifically, Post pregnancy depression, or postpartum depression, was not a highly recognized condition before, but due to many reported cases worldwide, it has come to light. Almost every ethnic group reports this issue for recovering mothers.
  • Medications- Medications are also one of the crucial reasons for developing depression. It may be acute or chronic, depending upon the continued use of the medication causing this side effect.

What symptoms to look for?

Usually, the symptoms don’t show up until significant progress has been made. Physiologically, the symptoms can manifest quite late. Psychologically, the onset is quite rapid, leaving little trails and clues for others to keenly pick up on. As a caregiver or concerned individual, a constant lookout for out-of-character talks and small changes can prompt you to initiate conversation.
According to one of the best depression doctor in Delhi, these are the symptoms to look out for-

  • Suddenly experiencing low energy throughout the day.
  • Feeling anxious without any reason, and the feeling lingers for more than a brief period.
  • Feeling disinterested in performing daily activities
  • The thoughts shared indicate sadness and an inability to cope with life
  • Reduced weight and appetite, with disinterest in eating for a long time
  • Shares feelings of being worthless and fixations on past problems
  • Chronic problems in sleeping (insomnia/hypersomnia)
  • Trouble concentrating on tasks at hand
  • Leaving things half-done and feeling irritated.

Based on these symptoms, the dominance of certain symptoms can describe the type of depression a person is experiencing. Based on it, further treatment plans can be evaluated.

  • Psychotherapy- It is considered as a first line of treatment for depressive disorders. If the condition dwindles between mild to moderate, it is a very effective method to explore. It uses interpersonal and cognitive behavioral therapy in its wake. It helps the clients to deal with their current problems, identify their negative beliefs and dominate them with a positive mindset, develop positive interactions with others, and reclaim the sense of satisfaction and control in their lives. It is necessary to administer since it raises realistic expectations towards life and develops the ability to handle distress.
  • Medications- If you approach a depression doctor in Delhi, it is possible that they may suggest a series of medications for providing treatment. These medications may include- -Selective Serotonin Uptake Inhibitors (SSRIs)- duloxetine, venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, and levomilnacipran -Atypical Antidepressants- Bupropion, Mirtazapine, Nefazodone etc. -Tricyclic antidepressants- Imipramine, doxepin, trimipramine, etc are not prescribed unless SSRIs have been used, since they tend to have a list of side effects. -Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)- These are prescribed when every other medication has failed to produce an effect. MAOIs include Tranylcypromine, Isocarboxazid etc. These are applicable in only certain cases due to the high level of side effects and deadly reactions to certain foods.
  • Non-traditional treatment- When people do not respond to traditional therapies and medications, alternate treatment methods are suggested. These include Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT) and Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) ECT uses high levels of electrical currents to stimulate nerves and release neurotransmitters to relieve depression, whereas TMS uses brief magnetic pulses to stimulate affected areas of the brain (responsible for mood regulation). It is used when antidepressants don’t show significant results.
