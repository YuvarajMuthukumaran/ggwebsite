Understanding Schizophrenia Treatment

The treatment of schizophrenia involves a mixture of psychopharmacological, psychological and counseling modalities to keep the symptoms in check and ease their effect on the client. The treatment regime continues even when the symptoms ease. Some may even require in-patient care if symptoms worsen.

A comprehensive care plan employs professionals like psychiatrists, psychologists, psychiatric nurses and social workers that keep a keen eye on the administration of treatment and ethical concerns associated with it.

Medications- Typically antipsychotics are prescribed to manage the symptoms. The prescription of doses is kept as low as possible to see its effects and avoid any side effects. Some common antipsychotics include-

First generation- Chlorpromazine, Haloperidol, Perphenazine, Fluphenazine

Second generation- Olanzapine, Clozapine, Aripiprazole, Risperidone and many others.

Psychosocial interventions-

It is important to undergo psychological therapies as well in order to recover and manage one’s emotion in a socially appropriate manner, since this condition can render people socially dysfunctional.

  • Individual Therapy- This is important to improve patterns of thought and help in dealing with irrational thought patterns. Learning to cope with stress and identifying patterns of onset of symptoms can be a great way of management.
  • Social skills training- It is an important part of dealing with schizophrenic patients. Since they are preoccupied with the issues of their perception and dealing with identifying real vs. false stimuli, they need to be trained accordingly to share their distress with others and find an immediate solution for it to alleviate their concerns.
  • Family therapy- Support from the family is necessary to manage the situations and stressors that one encounters during daily functioning.

Causes in development of Schizophrenia

There are no specific causes for development of schizophrenia. It is a mix of genetic mutations, brain chemistry and environmental triggers that leads to its onset.

Changes in neurotransmitters dopamine and glutamate have been observed to play a part in onset of schizophrenia. CNS and brain structures show significant changes when observed through neuroimaging techniques.
